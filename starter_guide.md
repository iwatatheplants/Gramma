### What to Do When You Get an Alert

**Speed is everything.** Have this workflow ready before your first alert hits:

1. **Open the listing immediately** — don't wait
2. **Check the photos** — look for brand labels, maker's marks, quality materials (see Part 2)
3. **Quick-check the value** — search the brand + item on 1stDibs, Chairish, or eBay "Sold" listings
4. **Message the seller within 60 seconds** using a pre-written template:

> "Hi! Is this still available? I can pick up today/tomorrow. Cash in hand. What time works for you?"

5. **Be flexible on pickup time** — the faster you can get there, the more deals you'll close
6. **Bring cash, a tape measure, moving blankets, and a friend for heavy items**

## PART 2: Brand & Designer Identification Guide

This is your competitive edge. Most people listing on Facebook Marketplace don't know what they have. You need to be able to spot value in seconds from a photo or in person.

### How to Identify Authentic Pieces

**Where to look for marks:**
- **Chairs:** Flip it over — check the underside of the seat, where legs meet the frame, and on the base. Look for paper labels, metal plates, or stamps.
- **Case goods (dressers, credenzas):** Pull out drawers — check the back panel, inside the top drawer, and the underside. Look for branded stamps, stickers, or foil labels.
- **Tables:** Check underneath the tabletop, especially near the edge or on the base/pedestal.
- **Upholstered furniture:** Check under cushions, look for tags sewn into the frame, and check the bottom dust cover.

**What to look for:**
- Manufacturer name, designer name, model number
- Production date or serial number
- "Made in Denmark" / "Made in USA" / "Made in Italy" stamps
- "Danish Control" or "Danish Furniture Makers" quality stamps
- Dovetail joints (hand-cut = older and more valuable)
- Solid wood vs. veneer vs. particle board (knock on it — solid wood sounds different)
- Tapered legs, clean lines, organic curves = likely mid-century

**Red flags for reproductions:**
- "Inspired by" or "in the style of" language
- Phillips head screws (originals often used slotted screws)
- Particle board or MDF construction
- Labels from modern retailers (Wayfair, Amazon, Target, etc.)
- Too-perfect condition on a supposedly 60-year-old piece

### Quick-Reference: Serial Number & Dating Tips

- **Herman Miller:** Look up serial numbers on their online archive to verify production dates
- **Knoll:** Digital database covers designs from mid-century to present
- **Fritz Hansen:** Provides authentication service through their website for Jacobsen and Wegner pieces
- **Lane Furniture:** Serial numbers encode the manufacture date in reverse — serial 258750 means it was made on May 7, 1985 (read the digits backward as a date)
- **Drexel/Broyhill:** Stamps inside drawers typically include collection name and date code
